*P4 deep dive — platform onboarding & activation nudges*

Built a second click-through, this one narrow instead of broad. It takes one line
off the P4 slide and follows it end to end for a single account, so the pillar
stops being a bullet and becomes something Trevor can argue with.

*The story*
Harlow Engineering Group: 5,000 employees, 2,000 logged into Melon. 40% adoption
looks like an engagement problem, so the instinct is to email the other 3,000
harder. It isn't. Six systems each hold one piece of who those people are and
none holds the whole picture. Once Data 360 resolves them into one profile per
employee, the 3,000 splits into four cohorts needing four different responses —
including 700 who should never be contacted, because they don't travel and never
will.

*The seven screens*
1. The gap — 40%, and three things Melon on its own cannot tell you
2. Six systems, six partial answers — Workday, Melon, Lumina, Agent Port, Mel, Mosaic
3. Unify and segment — the 3,000 breaks into 1,150 / 700 / 450 / 700, click any cohort
4. Mosaic + Data 360 — Mosaic decides what, Data 360 decides who, where and when
5. The nudge sequence — Mel push, then email built from her own bookings, then a consultant
6. Workspace — the consultant sees every message marketing sent, and activates on the call
7. Outcome — 40% to 78%, and nudges sent going *down* from 5,000 to 2,300

*The two lines that do the work*
"That join is the product" — the question "which employees travel but have never
used the platform?" cannot be asked of any single system. It needs Lumina's
bookings joined to Workday's roster, filtered by Melon's logins.

"The most valuable output isn't who to nudge, it's the 700 we now know to leave
alone." Every vendor promises better targeting. Almost nobody opens by handing
over a suppression list.

*What it covers off from the prep call*
• Mosaic gets its own screen — complementary, bidirectional, not a replacement
• Lumina, Agent Port and Mel all named properly as source systems
• Internal activation is a screen, not a footnote — Workspace consultant view
• The Azure objection gets answered by proxy on the Mosaic screen

*What it is not*
Hardcoded HTML and a JSON file. No org behind it, every number invented but
internally consistent, consoles are mock-ups, segment criteria is pseudo-SQL.
It shows what the capability looks like, not measured results. Doesn't change
the amber status — if anything it shows why it needs scoping.

*Two things to check before we use it*
1. Are Lumina / Agent Port / Mel / Mosaic named correctly? If not, it's wrong in
   the most visible place possible, in front of the person who owns them.
2. Harlow was 12 travellers in the broad walkthrough and is 5,000 employees here.
   I've bridged it as "renewal converted to a global mandate, 90 days on" and
   said so on screen 1, but it's the first thing a careful listener will notice.

*My honest read*
The best use may not be clicking through all seven. It might be showing screens
2 and 3, then putting the marker down and asking Trevor which of those cohorts he
recognises in his own data. Its job is to start the workshop, not be it.

Runs in a browser, single file, works offline. Happy to walk anyone through it,
or to park it — genuinely low cost either way.
